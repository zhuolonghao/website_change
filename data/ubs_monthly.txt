Sustaining resilience
Despite repeated shocks, from the pandemic and inflation to higher rates and technological disruption, global growth has endured, earnings have risen, and equity markets have hit record highs.
18 Nov 2026
Mark Haefele, Chief Investment Officer, Global Wealth Management
Recent years have repeatedly tested economies, companies, markets, and investors through a pandemic, energy shocks, higher inflation and interest rates, and rapid technological change. Yet global growth has held up, corporate earnings have risen, and equity markets have reached record highs.In our base case for 2027, we expect economic growth to remain close to trend and corporate earnings to rise further. We expect strong demand for computing capacity to support continued growth in AI investment, and moderating inflation should allow bond yields to decline modestly later in the year. We expect this backdrop to support further equity gains and positive fixed income returns, with structural demand also supporting broad commodities.But the range of potential outcomes is wide. In one risk scenario, sticky inflation, elevated government debt, and geopolitical risks could drive bond yields higher and equity markets lower. In another, mounting execution and monetization risks could erode investor confidence that strong AI demand will ultimately translate into attractive shareholder returns across the value chain.After a multi-year bull run, we therefore see a more balanced risk-reward for global equities in 2027. Our earnings forecasts remain constructive, but elevated expectations, index concentration, and uncertainty around the AI investment cycle leave less room for disappointment. We have therefore downgraded global equities from Attractive to Neutral.
