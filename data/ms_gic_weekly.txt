INVESTMENT STRATEGY
There is no better testament to the breadth and depth of this year’s
market resilience than small-cap US equities, with the Russell 2000
up 20%. Encouragingly, small caps have been supported by strong
fundamentals, as the asset class, leveraged to industrial strength, has
beneﬁted from ﬁscal stimulus and tariff rebates while outrunning
interest rate and oil price headwinds. Admittedly, the GIC got this one
wrong—underweighting the asset class over the past year given our
concerns around weak proﬁt dynamics and adverse selection in the
space after two decades of aggressive buying by private equity GPs.
But 6.5% nominal GDP growth lifts many boats, and here we are. That
said, is now the time to add small caps to portfolios? We remain
skeptical. Relative valuations have more than normalized and are now
extended; economic surprises have turned negative; and yield-curve
ﬂattening and/or delayed rate cuts are another potential headwind.
While selecting individual small caps may still work, the overall asset
class might only grind in line with the market from here. We prefer
focusing our risk budget on large-cap quality names that may better
withstand policy volatility and potential economic cooling. Consider
rebalancing ﬁxed income toward strategic benchmarks, neutralizing
tactical tilts, as we expect policy-related volatility to remain above
average. In equities, add to equal-weighted index exposure or active
stock selection. Financials, health care and some defensives ﬁt the
bill, along with select overweights to cloud-dominant hyperscalers.
We still like non-semiconductor-oriented EM and Japan and prefer
intermediate rates to credit, with added diversiﬁcation from hedge
funds, real assets, venture capital and growth equity.

earnings gains near 30%—a dynamic that embeds a two-turn compression of
valuation multiples. Nearly 70% of all large-cap S&P 500 constituents are trading

Improving breadth has led the equal-weighted S&P 500 to
outperform by approximately 250 basis points for the year to
date. Meanwhile, the price-momentum factor, which was a
dominant performance driver through June, has faded in
terms of leadership and given way to earnings-revision
momentum. The future, furthermore, looks solid, with 2027
GDP growth expected to remain resilient on the back of
continued investment growth emanating from the AI-
infrastructure build-out.
Thus far, there has been no better example of this breadth
and resilience than the performance of US small caps. As
measured by the Russell 2000 Index, they are up roughly
20% for the year to date, handily beating large caps. Indeed,
as strong as large-cap earnings have been year over year,
small-cap proﬁts have been even stronger, with operating
margins on pace to average 9.3%—more than 2.5 times the
2025 level. Inherently leveraged to economic growth, given
the index’s composition, small caps tilt toward ﬁnancials,
health care and industrials. They have outrun what we
thought would be headwinds from higher-for-longer front-
end rates and challenges of competing on scale and tech
innovation with larger-cap companies and those owned by
highly endowed private equity general partners (GPs). The
cohort has offset higher rates and the unanticipated impact of
increased oil prices, as better-than-expected manufacturing
strength, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and tariff rebates
have helped cushion results amid robust nominal GDP
growth, at around 6.5%. While the GIC underestimated this
performance and missed adding exposure a year ago, now the
question is, is it too late? We think it is.
First consider relative valuations. This year’s catch-up trade
has helped the Russell 2000 more than recover its historic
price/earnings premium. As seen in Chart of the Week, the
Russell 2000’s 29 times 12-month forward P/E ratio versus
the S&P 500’s 20 equates to a relative ratio above 1.45, well
over the 20-year average of 1.32 and in the 89th percentile of
occurrences. And 2027 earnings estimates are ambitious,
embedding another 250 basis points of operating-margin
improvement in earnings forecast to be an extraordinary 50%
higher than at year-end 2025.
We consider these expectations to be challenged by a second
reality, in the form of indications of cyclical economic slowing.
Already, the Citi Economic Surprise Index, typically strongly
correlated with the Russell 2000, has rolled over hard, with
readings lately around 25 versus its June peak in the 60s.
Industrial and manufacturing purchasing managers’ indexes
(PMIs) recently missed expectations and decelerated, while
nondefense capital goods orders weakened meaningfully in
July, to only 0.2% month over month versus expectations of
0.7%. Conference Board measures of consumer conﬁdence
were also softer than hoped for. Ultimately, while we expect
2027 real economic growth to remain solid, at around 2.0%
to 2.5%, nominal growth is expected to become much slower,
clocking in closer to 4.5%–5.0%.
A ﬁnal point pertains to rates, the US dollar and commodities.
Heretofore, small caps have shrugged off developments
around Federal Reserve rate-cut expectations and weathered
rate-hike potential priced by fed funds futures. With many
small caps dependent on ﬂoating-rate front-end ﬁnancing, the
50-basis-point two-year US Treasury yield backup since mid-
April is signiﬁcant. Equally, Treasury Secretary Bessent’s
potential bond market intervention to ﬂatten the yield curve
could foster headwinds to banks’ proﬁts and willingness to
lend. Bessent’s actions also suggest dollar debasement, and
the greenback is now approximately 2% weaker than in June.
This adds inﬂation pressure to imported input and commodity
prices, which many smaller ﬁrms can’t offset with
international sales revenues. Finally, there are the pressures
from higher-for-longer oil and energy prices. It remains to be
seen, which small-cap companies can sustain margin
momentum against these headwinds.
Bottom Line. A year ago, the GIC made an active decision not
to overweight small-cap US stocks in our diversiﬁed
portfolios, underestimating the magnitude and breadth of
the cyclical rebound in 2026 and overestimating obstacles
to strong operating-leverage and proﬁt recovery. We opted
to spend our risk budget on real assets, emerging markets
and hedge funds, which has been accretive to total returns.
But we were wrong versus the S&P 500, as the Russell
2000 has bested the S&P 500 this year. While we
continually analyze our choices and process, we have to
invest looking forward in the context of a fully diversiﬁed
portfolio. While small-cap technicals and revisions
momentum are encouraging, we are not prepared to add to
small caps now in a capitulative chase. Relative valuations
versus higher-quality large caps have recovered from their
post-COVID malaise; consensus forecasts imply an ambitious
2027 jump in margins just as manufacturing PMIs show signs
of cooling; and rate-hike risk continues—all headwinds,
especially if tariff uncertainty and higher energy prices
persist. We prefer large-cap quality for the year ahead.
Watch economic surprises and cyclical strength indicators as
opportunities to rightsize small-cap exposure. Consider
rebalancing ﬁxed income toward strategic benchmarks,
neutralizing tactical tilts, as we expect policy-related
volatility to remain above average. In equities, add to equal-
weighted index exposure or active stock selection. Financials,
health care and some defensives ﬁt the bill, along with
select overweights to cloud-dominant hyperscalers. We still
like non-semiconductor-oriented EM and Japan and prefer
intermediate rates to credit, with added diversiﬁcation from
hedge funds, real assets, venture capital and growth equity.

Chart of the Week: Small-Cap Relative Value Has Normalized
After lagging from mid-2022 to mid-2025, the small-cap
Russell 2000 has outpaced the S&P 500 in the past 12
months and for the year to date. In fact, the year-to-date
performance gap is over 700 basis points. The move,
coinciding with cyclicals outperformance of defensives
and improved market breadth, has been ratiﬁed by a
rebound in small-cap earnings growth. Now, relative
valuations have normalized, rising above the 20-year
average. Small caps have been resilient amid higher rates
and oil prices, as they have been buttressed by tax reform
and tariff rebates. But without valuation support and with
economic momentum potentially stalling, headwinds like
higher rates and inputs costs, exacerbated by US dollar
weakening, may inhibit the small-cap premium. We would be rebalancing and taking proﬁts, while redeploying to larger-cap
quality among select hyperscalers, ﬁnancials, health care, energy/commodities and infrastructure.
Fixed Income Insight: Price-Insensitive Buyers’ Exit Pressures Term Premiums
Bond investors have continued to muse about Secretary
Bessent’s intentions on long-maturity Treasury buyouts.
While rhetoric around potential for even more purchases
than the announced level recently drove 20-year-plus
yields down around 15 basis points and reduced short
exposure, the plan to suppress yields may face structural
headwinds. Historically price-insensitive buyers like the
Fed itself and other central banks have reduced their
share of holdings from 46% ﬁve years ago to 35%. This
has occurred as natural duration buyers—pension funds
and insurers—have shifted toward higher-risk areas,
including private credit. Notably, this doesn’t consider
market competition from AI-linked and global sovereign
issuers. As the investor base shifts, it is natural for term premiums demanded to rise. We’re not sure Secretary Bessant can ﬁx
that, especially if the US’ relative ﬁnancials continue to deteriorate under the weight of uncontrolled debt and deﬁcits.

Tactical Asset Allocation Reasoning
Global Equities Weight Relative to
Model Benchmark
 
US Overweight
The productivity-boom thesis catalyzed by the GenAI-capex build-out, along with aggressive ﬁscal
and regulatory stimulus, has become very much consensus. Forecasts are ambitious and embed an
expectation for cyclical broadening that takes "S&P 493" earnings growth from 6%–8% in 2025 to
24%–26% in 2026. In a market that is already expensive, concentrated and complacent, this
suggests the aperture for upside surprise is narrow, and markets are apt to be quite brittle in the
face of exogenous shocks. With midterm-election-related policy already hitting a fever pitch,
potential for these undiscounted events to occur is rising. Thus, while we are bullish (with MS &
Co.'s 2Q2027 S&P 500 Index target at 8,300), we prefer stock-picking to simply owning the cap-
weighted index. Favor ﬁnancials, health care, select industrials and energy.
International Equities
(Developed Markets) Underweight
Fiscal stimulus, a response to the "America First" agenda, has helped to drive recent
outperformance. This is creating rest-of-world (ROW) opportunities that may simultaneously
enjoy monetary, ﬁscal and currency-related stimulus. The outlook is improving in Japan, as
corporate restructuring occurs alongside a reﬂationary surge three decades in the making.
Emerging Markets Overweight
The "new Monroe Doctrine" creates opportunities not only for pro-business political stability in
Latin America, but also for direct investment. For Asia, strategic realignment opportunities
continue to surface, as the US turns away and the US-China trade conﬂict has effectively reached
a one-year truce, fostering opportunities. India remains a recommended secular growth long. The
GIC reduced its overweight after a signiﬁcant rally in semiconductor names, which skewed index
composition.
Global Fixed Income Weight Relative to
Model Benchmark
 
US Investment Grade Underweight
The GIC maintains close-to-neutral views in investment grade. In the ﬁrst half of 2026, solid
growth, supported by ﬁscal stimulus, and inﬂationary pressures supported a higher-for-longer
interest rate environment. Should the Fed maintain a "hold" versus enacting a hike as its next
move, investors could ﬁnd some value in IG. As a point of caution, we do anticipate a surge in IG
corporate issuance, on the back of increasing capex and rising M&A deal activity. Given the
picture, we recommend limited short-duration exposure and moving toward the “belly of the
curve” to capture decent coupons with lower price volatility. We see the long end continuing to
be plagued by structural imbalances that show up as widening term premiums.
Opportunistic Fixed
Income Market-Weight
High yield is competing effectively, with better liquidity, greater transparency and more favorable
credit quality than private credit. In ROW and emerging market debt, yields are decent, and there
may be modest room for spread tightening, as economic growth improves.
Alternative Investments Weight Relative to
Model Benchmark
 
Real Assets Overweight
We expect higher stock-bond correlations, which place a premium on the diversiﬁcation beneﬁts
of investing in real assets. Nevertheless, with real interest rates positive and services inﬂation
remaining quite sticky, we would need to be selective in adding to this asset class broadly. We are
focused on industrial metals, energy infrastructure and interesting opportunities aimed at solving
the residential housing shortage. 
Hedged Strategies Overweight
We added to equity hedged positions early in 2026, noting the pickup in idiosyncratic risk and
rising stock level dispersion. The current environment appears constructive for hedge fund
managers, who are frequently good stock-pickers and can use leverage and risk management to
potentially amplify returns. We prefer very active and fundamental strategies, especially high-
quality, low-beta, low-volatility and absolute return/market neutral strategies.

Glossary
Drawdown refers to the largest cumulative percentage decline in net asset value or the percentage decline from the highest value or net asset
value (peak) to the lowest value net asset value (trough) after the peak.
Earnings revisions breadth is deﬁned as the number of positive analyst revisions minus the number of negative analyst revisions divided by the
total number of revisions.
Equity risk premium is the excess return that an individual stock or the overall stock market provides over a risk-free rate. The risk-free rate
represents the interest an investor would expect from an absolutely risk-free investment over a speciﬁed period of time.
Illiquidity premium is the extra yield investors expect to earn for giving up control to liquidate their capital for a certain period of time.
M2 is a measure of the money supply that includes all elements of M1 as well as "near money." M1 includes cash and checking deposits, while
near money refers to savings deposits, money market securities, mutual funds and other time deposits.
Nominal Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is the GDP of the country measured at current market prices and not adjusted for inﬂation or
deﬂation.
Option-adjusted spread (OAS) is a measurement of the spread of a ﬁxed income security rate and the risk-free rate of return, which is adjusted
to take into account an embedded option.
Price to forward earnings calculates the price-to-earnings ratio that uses projected future earnings.
R-star is the real short-term interest rate expected to prevail when an economy is at full strength and inﬂation is stable.
Risk premium is the return in excess of the risk-free rate of return an investment is expected to yield.
Term premium is the excess yield that investors require to commit to holding a long-term bond instead of a series of shorter-term bonds.
Volatility This is a statistical measure of the dispersion of returns for a given security or market index. Volatility can either be measured by using
the standard deviation or variance between returns from that same security or market index. Commonly, the higher the volatility, the riskier the
security.

MLPs
Master Limited Partnerships (MLPs) are limited partnerships or limited liability companies that are taxed as partnerships and whose interests
(limited partnership units or limited liability company units) are traded on securities exchanges like shares of common stock. Currently, most
MLPs operate in the energy, natural resources or real estate sectors. Investments in MLP interests are subject to the risks generally applicable
to companies in the energy and natural resources sectors, including commodity pricing risk, supply and demand risk, depletion risk and
exploration risk. Individual MLPs are publicly traded partnerships that have unique risks related to their structure. These include, but are not
limited to, their reliance on the capital markets to fund growth, adverse ruling on the current tax treatment of distributions (typically mostly
tax deferred), and commodity volume risk. The potential tax beneﬁts from investing in MLPs depend on their being treated as partnerships for
federal income tax purposes and, if the MLP is deemed to be a corporation, then its income would be subject to federal taxation at the entity
level, reducing the amount of cash available for distribution to the fund which could result in a reduction of the fund’s value.
MLPs carry interest rate risk and may underperform in a rising interest rate environment. MLP funds accrue deferred income taxes for future tax
liabilities associated with the portion of MLP distributions considered to be a tax-deferred return of capital and for any net operating gains as
well as capital appreciation of its investments; this deferred tax liability is reﬂected in the daily NAV; and, as a result, the MLP fund’s after-tax
performance could differ signiﬁcantly from the underlying assets even if the pre-tax performance is closely tracked.

Bonds are subject to interest rate risk. When interest rates rise, bond prices fall; generally the longer a bond's maturity, the more sensitive it is

Ultrashort bond funds Ultra-short bond funds are mutual funds and exchange-traded funds that generally invest in ﬁxed income securities with
very short maturities, typically less than one year. They are not money market funds. While money market funds attempt to maintain a stable
net asset value, an ultra-short bond fund’s net asset value will ﬂuctuate, which may result in the loss of the principal amount invested. They are
therefore subject to the risks associated with debt securities such as credit and interest rate risk.
Ultrashort-term ﬁxed income asset class is comprised of ﬁxed income securities with high quality, very short maturities. They are therefore
subject to the risks associated with debt securities such as credit and interest rate risk.
Duration, the most commonly used measure of bond risk, quantiﬁes the effect of changes in interest rates on the price of a bond or bond
portfolio. The longer the duration, the more sensitive the bond or portfolio would be to changes in interest rates. Generally, if interest rates
rise, bond prices fall and vice versa. Longer-term bonds carry a longer or higher duration than shorter-term bonds; as such, they would be
affected by changing interest rates for a greater period of time if interest rates were to increase. Consequently, the price of a long-term bond
would drop signiﬁcantly as compared to the price of a short-term bond.
