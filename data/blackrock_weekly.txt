Camera frame
Title slide: Inflation: beyond the Mideast energy shock
We think the impact of the Middle East energy shock on U.S. inflation has peaked.
But with the economy still strong and core price pressures lingering, the outlook isn't straightforward.
1: The energy aftershock
Oil and fuel prices have fallen sharply this month. We expect that to help bring headline inflation down next year, reversing much of the earlier surge. But not all price pressures are fading. Strong wage growth is still keeping the cost of core services inflation elevated.
2: Growth holds up
The U.S. economy remains resilient, supported by solid hiring and wage growth.
We expect growth to moderate next year, but stay above 2%. Meanwhile, falling energy prices and slower AI capital spending growth could help ease inflation pressures.
3: The Fed’s path
Markets expect as many as three Fed rate hikes over the next year.
We think that's overdone. We expect one quarter-point hike in December and another in the first half of 2027. Overall, we expect fewer hikes than markets currently expect.
Outro: Here’s our Market take
We expect U.S. growth to hold up as inflation cools, even if some core price pressures persist. We favor short- and medium-term U.S. government bonds for attractive income and more protection against rate swings in this environment.
Closing frame: Read details: blackrock.com/weekly-commentary
Inflation's outlook
We see headline inflation cooling in 2027, even as markets price in more Fed hikes than we expect. We favor short- and medium-term U.S. Treasuries.
Market Backdrop
The Nasdaq plunged 10% into bear market territory last week as solid Nvidia earnings and Anthropic’s record IPO failed to lift AI sentiment.
Week ahead
We eye October PCE data to assess whether services inflation remains a key sticking point – and if the figures keep further Fed tightening in play.
Markets are largely pricing in a December Federal Reserve rate hike, in line with our view, plus as many as two more next year. We think the latter is overdone. We expect headline U.S. inflation to cool next year while economic growth moderates as oil prices, the effects of tariffs and AI-related price pressures ease. That gives the Fed room to tighten less than markets expect. We favor short- and medium-term U.S. Treasuries for solid income and greater protection against rate swings.

The energy aftershock

We think the energy shock's impact on U.S. inflation has peaked. Brent crude oil, gasoline and diesel prices have all fallen sharply this month, which should help ease headline inflation and core inflation through lower prices for transportation services. We estimate the shock added 1.5 percentage points to headline U.S. inflation at its September peak. That contribution should reverse, from boosting inflation this year to lowering it by 0.8 percentage points by year-end 2027. See the chart. While price pressures from tariffs and the AI buildout should ease next year, that relief will be uneven. In Europe, earlier increases in natural gas prices are still feeding through to consumers, prolonging inflationary pressures relative to the U.S. In the U.S., a tight labor market is stoking wage growth, keeping core services inflation elevated even as energy price pressures abate.
The economy is currently strong, while the labor market remains tight and core inflation is elevated. Against that backdrop, we expect core inflation to remain closer to 3% than the Fed’s 2% target. That supports further Fed tightening and suggests the neutral interest rate may be higher than previously assumed – consistent with the new regime we’ve long described. Yet we think markets have recently extrapolated today's economic strength and inflation pressures too far and priced in more Fed rate hikes than will be needed. Falling energy prices reduce the risk of lasting effects on core inflation, while tighter financial conditions and uncertainty around the AI buildout could temper demand and hiring in 2027 and, with it, core inflation.
A too hawkish read
We expect U.S. growth to moderate next year, even as the economy remains resilient. Growth is on track to reach 2% to 2.5% this year. Solid hiring and wage growth of around 4% – above the 2%–2.5% pace we see as consistent with the Fed’s target – point to strength beyond AI. With the breakeven pace of job creation now much lower, even modest hiring keeps the labor market tight. We see growth moderating but staying above 2% next year, as AI spending grows at a slower pace and the boost from President Donald Trump's tax cuts fades. The U.S. remains better placed than Europe to withstand the energy shock because it is a net energy exporter with more limited exposure to fluctuations in natural gas prices. That outlook underpins our expectation for a more measured Fed rate path. Market pricing suggests as many as three quarter-point hikes over the next 12 months. We think a quarter-point hike in December is likely and we currently expect one more hike in the first half of 2027. We also expect the European Central Bank to raise rates next month, but the outlook beyond December depends on whether energy-driven price pressures keep feeding through to underlying inflation.
With two- and 10-year U.S. Treasury yields hitting multidecade highs this year, much of the structural rise in rates we have long anticipated is now reflected in bond yields. That underscores our preference for short- and medium-term Treasuries, which offer attractive income opportunities and more protection against higher rates. We recently strengthened our strategic overweight on inflation-linked bonds to hedge against supply-driven inflation risks.
Our bottom line
We expect U.S. growth to hold steady and headline inflation to cool next year, even as core services price pressures remain elevated. We favor short- and medium-term U.S. Treasuries for solid income.
Market backdrop
Anthropic’s record debut and Nvidia earnings failed to lift AI sentiment last week. The Nasdaq slid 10%, notched its worst week since the Covid-19 pandemic and slipped into bear market territory. The S&P 500 fell 9% and confirmed a correction. The sell-off suggests concerns around company-specific CEO succession and circular financing deals persist. U.S. 10-year Treasury yields fell to 4.94%, about 40 basis points below October’s 24-year high. Two-year yields slipped to 4.64%.
We’re watching U.S. PCE data – the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge – for signs that persistent services inflation is keeping further Fed tightening in play. U.S. consumer confidence and the second estimate of GDP growth will offer a check on the resilience of the broader economy beyond the AI boom, while global flash PMIs will provide further insight into growth momentum.

Intersecting mega forces
Since we launched our mega forces framework it has become clearer how their intersection shapes almost all our investment views and opens up alpha opportunities. They cut across asset class labels, spurring a rethink of portfolio construction. Investors need to be deliberate about the economic or thematic exposures they own, the vehicles they use to implement them and their investment horizons.

Six to 12-month tactical views on selected assets vs. broad global asset classes by level of conviction, November 2026
We have lengthened our tactical investment horizon back to six to 12 months. The table below reflects this and, importantly, leaves aside the opportunity for alpha, or the potential to generate above-benchmark returns – especially at a time of heightened volatility.

Equities
Europe ex UK
We are neutral. We would need to see more business-friendly policy and deeper capital markets for Europe to outperform and to justify a broad overweight. We stay selective, favoring financials, infrastructure, industrials, materials and healthcare.
Germany
We are neutral. Higher spending on defense and infrastructure support the corporate sector. Valuations are not stretched but neither compelling and expected earnings growth lags other countries. Light positioning means potential easing in geopolitical tensions and AI lifting industrials-driven earnings could create opportunities.
France
We are neutral. Strong expected earnings growth and global revenue exposure support French corporates. Valuations are less compelling, albeit not stretched versus euro area peers. Persistent political uncertainty leaves the overall risk reward balanced.
Italy
We are neutral. Earnings growth and momentum are strong, boosted by the significant exposure to financials, utilities and energy. Valuations are still at discount vs. peers, but not as much as in previous years. Political risk is currently low but likely to pick up ahead of 2027 elections.
Spain
We are overweight. Valuations remain attractive vs. peers even if relative earnings momentum has slowed. Strong domestic demand growth and exposure to fast-growing areas like Latin America support Spanish stocks. Financials, utilities and infrastructure-linked stocks would benefit from Europe’s push towards autonomy.
Netherlands
We are neutral. Earnings revisions in the IT sector, a large sector in the Dutch stock market is offset by other sectors seeing less favorable valuations and a weaker earnings outlook than European peers.
Switzerland
We are neutral. Valuations have improved, from stretched levels, but the earnings outlook is weaker than other European markets. If global risk appetite stays strong, the index’s tilt to stable, less volatile sectors may weigh on performance .
UK
We are neutral. Valuations remain attractive relative to the U.S., but we see few near-term catalysts to trigger a shift.
Fixed income
Euro area government bonds
We are neutral short- and medium-term bonds. Current ECB rate pricing looks fairly valued given the balance of risks. Higher energy prices could push rates higher. We prefer to deploy risk elsewhere.
German bunds
We are neutral Bunds. Current ECB rate pricing looks more fairly valued given the balance of risks. Fiscal stimulus and increased bond issuance exert upward pressure on yields, alongside inflation risks amid lingering geopolitical tensions.
French OATs
We are neutral. Elevated political uncertainty, high budget deficits and slow structural reforms could stoke volatility, but these risks already seem priced into OATs and we don’t expect a material worsening from here.
Italian BTPs
We are neutral. The spread over German bunds looks tight given Italy’s large budget deficits and growing public debt. Domestic factors remain supportive, with growth holding up relative to the rest of the euro area and local demand for BTPs solid at current yield levels. Domestic disapproval would likely prevent defense spending from reaching fiscally unstable levels. Political uncertainty is likely to increase ahead of next year’s elections.
UK gilts
We are neutral. We expect periods of elevated volatility given political uncertainty, longer-term bonds making up a larger market share, and buyers becoming more price-sensitive.
Swiss government bonds
We are neutral. The SNB seems comfortable with its medium-term inflation outlook and its current policy stance. Market pricing is broadly in line with SNB messaging.
European inflation-protected securities
We are neutral. We see higher medium-term inflation, but inflation expectations are firmly anchored. Cooling inflation and uncertain growth may matter more near term.
European investment grade
European investment grade is supported by healthy corporate sector balance sheets, contained default rates and the persistently strong demand for durable income from European households. We prefer European investment grade over the U.S.
European high yield
We are overweight. While spreads are low, the income potential remains attractive. Defaults are contained and high yield is of higher quality and less sensitive to interest rate swings compared with the U.S.
