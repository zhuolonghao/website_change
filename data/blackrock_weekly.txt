Title slide: Upgrading U.S. Treasuries to lock in income
This week we are making some tactical view changes, in particular changing our views on U.S. Treasuries and U.S. equities.
1: Locking in U.S. Treasury income
Starting with U.S. Treasuries, we first went underweight U.S. long-duration Treasuries back in October 2020, when the U.S. 10-year [yield] was still below 0.9%.
Since then, we have had brief periods of neutral but stayed mostly underweight.
The U.S. 10-year [yield] is now at five and a quarter [5.25%], and we are upgrading to neutral to lock in income.
We’re refraining from making a bigger bullish call on the long end of the U.S. Treasuries market and instead are upgrading front-end Treasuries into overweight, given the greater income buffer for further yield increases.
2: Downgrading U.S. equities
On U.S. equities, we are bringing overweight to neutral, partly funding the Treasuries upgrade.
We still prefer AI and tech within the neutral stance.
This is a short-term risk management move, really reflecting the fact that we believe investors need to be more dynamic in this new market and macro regime.
3: A more dynamic approach
Our conviction in the fundamentals remains intact, and we stand ready to add risk on potentially more attractive venture points.
Other adjustments include trimming our Japanese government bond underweight, after Japanese 10-year [yields] rose from [around] 0.4% when we first initiated the position in mid-2023 to now around 3%.
Outro: Here’s our Market take
The big picture is that the new investment regime requires a very dynamic approach.
We are managing risk, not changing our core convictions.
And we’re also monitoring growing population focus on AI policy and AI buildout.
Closing frame: Read details: blackrock.com/weekly-commentary
Income in focus
We upgrade U.S. Treasuries, preferring short-term bonds, as higher yields improve potential returns, partly funded by downgrading U.S. equities to neutral.
Market backdrop
Global bonds swung sharply last week, with 10- and 30-year U.S. Treasury yields hitting 24-year highs before retreating after strong bond auctions.
Week ahead
U.S. CPI and PPI offer the first look at September inflation this week. We watch for signs that could shift Fed expectations and Treasury yields.
Higher yields and potentially excessive Fed tightening expectations create room to unwind our U.S. Treasury underweight – a mainstay for most of the last six years. We upgrade Treasuries, favoring short-term bonds. In turn, we take profits: downgrading U.S. equities to neutral to manage potential volatility around the AI buildout and trimming our Japanese government bond (JGB) underweight. We retain our conviction in AI fundamentals and preference for AI within U.S. equities.

The 10-year Treasury yield has risen 110 basis points this year to around 5.25%, driven mainly by higher real interest rates rather than inflation expectations or term premium. Two forces are behind the rise in real rates: intensifying competition for capital is pushing up the neutral rate while Middle East tensions and higher oil prices have driven a sharp reversal in Fed rate expectations. Since January, markets have priced in 160 additional basis points of Fed tightening over the next 12 months. We think that is overdone. With term premium averaging around 75 basis points since September, higher yields offer a more attractive entry point. At current yields, our analysis suggests a 75-basis-point cushion against further yield increases over 12 months before total returns turn negative. That supports adding U.S. Treasuries, especially at the short end, where we see the greatest scope for Fed tightening expectations to unwind.
We are adding Treasuries from across the curve. For most of the last six years, we’ve been underweight Treasuries, turning neutral in spots. Now, yields have reset toward our long-held higher-rates view, and we want to lock in some attractive income. Ten-year Treasury yields sat near 0.9% back in October 2020 when we first went underweight and have since surged to 24-year highs above 5.3%. We are upgrading shorter-term Treasuries to overweight, where we see scope for excessive Fed tightening expectations to unwind, and long-term Treasuries to neutral as higher yields now provide better compensation for interest-rate risk. We also trim our underweight in JGBs, held since July 2023 when 10-year yields were at 0.4%-0.5%, compared with above 3% now. We fund the Treasury upgrades partly from U.S. agency mortgage-backed securities that have been a stable source of income in portfolios and we are now neutral.
Downgrading U.S. equities
We also fund the Treasury upgrades by closing our long-held U.S. equity overweight to neutral. This is a short-term risk-management move, reflecting our belief that investors must be more dynamic in today’s volatile macro and market regime. We are also monitoring growing population focus on AI policy and the AI buildout in jurisdictions across the country.
Our fundamental convictions on AI transformation remain intact. Higher rates are increasingly differentiating companies by their ability to generate earnings and returns above the cost of capital. We still favor AI-related companies and listed infrastructure, where growing demand and scarcity in critical infrastructure support attractive returns. Existing infrastructure could grow even more valuable amid this scarcity. Despite narrative swings, we believe fundamentals will ultimately prevail. We also maintain our emerging market equity overweight. FactSet data shows third-quarter earnings for S&P 500 companies are expected to grow nearly 30% year-on-year.
Our bottom line
We are managing near-term risk, not changing our core convictions. Higher yields offer an opportunity to add Treasuries, funded partly by taking profits on U.S. equities. We remain constructive on AI and stand ready to add risk again as visibility improves and attractive entry points emerge.
Market backdrop
Global government bonds saw another volatile week, with 10- and 30-year U.S. Treasury yields hitting their highest levels since 2002 before retreating after strong bond auctions. European bonds also sold off, led by France amid fiscal concerns. Oil prices rose as Middle East tensions persisted, adding to inflation worries. We see the bond selloff as reinforcing our competition-for-capital thesis, while higher yields improve the case for owning Treasuries.
U.S. CPI and PPI data take center stage this week, offering the first snapshots of September inflation. Fed minutes last week underscored caution about the policy path and the importance of incoming data. We are watching for persistent price pressures amid higher energy costs and recent PCE methodology changes, with the readings likely to shape Fed rate expectations and Treasury yields.

Intersecting mega forces
Since we launched our mega forces framework it has become clearer how their intersection shapes almost all our investment views and opens up alpha opportunities. They cut across asset class labels, spurring a rethink of portfolio construction. Investors need to be deliberate about the economic or thematic exposures they own, the vehicles they use to implement them and their investment horizons.

Euro-denominated tactical granular views
Six to 12-month tactical views on selected assets vs. broad global asset classes by level of conviction, October 2026
We have lengthened our tactical investment horizon back to six to 12 months. The table below reflects this and, importantly, leaves aside the opportunity for alpha, or the potential to generate above-benchmark returns – especially at a time of heightened volatility.

Tactical view
Commentary
Equities
Europe ex UK
We are neutral. We would need to see more business-friendly policy and deeper capital markets for Europe to outperform and to justify a broad overweight. We stay selective, favoring financials, infrastructure, industrials, materials and healthcare.
Germany
We are neutral. Higher spending on defense and infrastructure support the corporate sector. Valuations are not stretched but neither compelling and expected earnings growth lags other countries. Light positioning means potential easing in geopolitical tensions and AI lifting industrials-driven earnings could create opportunities.
France
We are neutral. Strong expected earnings growth and global revenue exposure support French corporates. Valuations are less compelling, albeit not stretched versus euro area peers. Persistent political uncertainty leaves the overall risk reward balanced.
Italy
We are neutral. Earnings growth and momentum are strong, boosted by the significant exposure to financials, utilities and energy. Valuations are still at discount vs. peers, but not as much as in previous years. Political risk is currently low but likely to pick up ahead of 2027 elections.
Spain
We are overweight. Valuations remain attractive vs. peers even if relative earnings momentum has slowed. Strong domestic demand growth and exposure to fast-growing areas like Latin America support Spanish stocks. Financials, utilities and infrastructure-linked stocks would benefit from Europe’s push towards autonomy.
Netherlands
We are neutral. Earnings revisions in the IT sector, a large sector in the Dutch stock market is offset by other sectors seeing less favorable valuations and a weaker earnings outlook than European peers.
Switzerland
We are neutral. Valuations have improved, from stretched levels, but the earnings outlook is weaker than other European markets. If global risk appetite stays strong, the index’s tilt to stable, less volatile sectors may weigh on performance .
UK
We are neutral. Valuations remain attractive relative to the U.S., but we see few near-term catalysts to trigger a shift.
Fixed income
Euro area government bonds
We are neutral short- and medium-term bonds. Current ECB rate pricing looks fairly valued given the balance of risks. Higher energy prices could push rates higher. We prefer to deploy risk elsewhere.
German bunds
We are neutral Bunds. Current ECB rate pricing looks more fairly valued given the balance of risks. Fiscal stimulus and increased bond issuance exert upward pressure on yields, alongside inflation risks amid lingering geopolitical tensions.
French OATs
We are neutral. Elevated political uncertainty, high budget deficits and slow structural reforms could stoke volatility, but these risks already seem priced into OATs and we don’t expect a material worsening from here.
Italian BTPs
We are neutral. The spread over German bunds looks tight given Italy’s large budget deficits and growing public debt. Domestic factors remain supportive, with growth holding up relative to the rest of the euro area and local demand for BTPs solid at current yield levels. Domestic disapproval would likely prevent defense spending from reaching fiscally unstable levels. Political uncertainty is likely to increase ahead of next year’s elections.
UK gilts
We are neutral. We expect periods of elevated volatility given political uncertainty, longer-term bonds making up a larger market share, and buyers becoming more price-sensitive.
Swiss government bonds
We are neutral. The SNB seems comfortable with its medium-term inflation outlook and its current policy stance. Market pricing is broadly in line with SNB messaging.
European inflation-protected securities
We are neutral. We see higher medium-term inflation, but inflation expectations are firmly anchored. Cooling inflation and uncertain growth may matter more near term.
European investment grade
European investment grade is supported by healthy corporate sector balance sheets, contained default rates and the persistently strong demand for durable income from European households. We prefer European investment grade over the U.S.
European high yield
We are overweight. While spreads are low, the income potential remains attractive. Defaults are contained and high yield is of higher quality and less sensitive to interest rate swings compared with the U.S.
